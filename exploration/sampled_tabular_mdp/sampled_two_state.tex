\section{Research path}
Cesani, Papini, and Restelli prove for a reduced categorical softmax bandit
\begin{equation}
\log\det F_{\mathrm{red}}=\sum_a\log\pi(a),
\qquad \nabla\log\det F_{\mathrm{red}}=\mathbf 1-K\pi.
\end{equation}
Step 1 verified this identity, Step 2 added stochastic bandit rewards, and
Step 3 introduced state visitation while keeping every expectation exact.
Step 4 replaces population expectations by finite trajectory batches. This
isolates estimator bias, variance, rank loss, and missing-state batches before
shared neural parameters are introduced \cite{cesani2026}.

\section{Controlled MDP}
All trajectories start at $s_0$. At $s_0$, $a_0$ terminates with reward $0.5$,
$a_1$ reaches $s_1$ with reward zero, and $a_2$ terminates with reward zero.
At $s_1$, actions terminate with rewards $(1,0.2,0)$. The horizon is two,
$\gamma=1$, and each state uses two reduced logits with $a_2$ as reference.
Writing $q=\pi_0(a_1)$,
\begin{equation}
J(\phi)=0.5\pi_0(a_0)+q[\pi_1(a_0)+0.2\pi_1(a_1)].
\end{equation}
This separates reaching $s_1$ from behaving well after reaching it.

\section{Population geometry from Step 3}
An infinite transition-pooled dataset has
\begin{equation}
\mu_0=\frac{1}{1+q},\qquad \mu_1=\frac{q}{1+q},\qquad
F_{\mathrm{pool}}=\operatorname{blockdiag}(\mu_0F_0,\mu_1F_1).
\end{equation}
With $B_s=\sum_a\log\pi_s(a)$,
\begin{align}
\log\det F_{\mathrm{pool}}&=B_0+B_1+2\log\mu_0+2\log\mu_1,\\
B_{\mathrm{full}}&=\tfrac12\log\det F_{\mathrm{pool}}
=\tfrac12(B_0+B_1)+\log\mu_0+\log\mu_1.
\end{align}
The factor $1/2$ is an action-matched convention, not a canonical one.

\section{Finite-batch transition pooling}
For $N$ trajectories, define
\begin{equation}
X_i=\mathbf1\{\text{trajectory }i\text{ reaches }s_1\},\qquad
K_1=\sum_iX_i\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(N,q).
\end{equation}
There are $M=N+K_1$ valid transitions and
\begin{equation}
\widehat\mu_0=\frac{N}{N+K_1},\qquad
\widehat\mu_1=\frac{K_1}{N+K_1}.
\end{equation}
The denominator is random, so at finite $N$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[\widehat\mu_1]\ne\frac{q}{1+q},
\qquad \Pr(K_1=0)=(1-q)^N.
\end{equation}
When $K_1=0$, the sampled barrier has no $s_1$ component, the empirical Fisher
has a zero $F_1$ block, and its log-determinant is undefined. No pseudocount or
spectral floor hides this event.

\section{Sampled estimators}
The primary reward estimator is raw reward-to-go REINFORCE \cite{Williams1992}:
\begin{equation}
\widehat g_J=\frac1N\sum_i\sum_tG_{i,t}
\nabla_\phi\log\pi_\phi(A_{i,t}\mid S_{i,t}).
\end{equation}
It averages complete trajectory contributions. Centering and normalization
are disabled in the primary result.

The practical categorical barrier is
\begin{equation}
\widehat g_{\mathrm{cond}}=
\widehat\mu_0\nabla B_0+\widehat\mu_1\nabla B_1.
\end{equation}
It treats sampled states as fixed and therefore omits visitation derivatives.

One score row is formed for every valid sampled transition:
\begin{equation}
S_{m,:}=\nabla_\phi\log\pi_\phi(a_m\mid s_m)^{\top},
\qquad \widehat F=\frac1M S^{\top}S.
\end{equation}
This Fisher is undamped and may be rank deficient. It is not the reward
Hessian and is not automatically interchangeable with the population Fisher
\cite{kunstner2019}.

Every curve uses a sampled reward gradient. Six retain Step 3 regularizers as
population-oracle controls; the seventh is the practical estimator.

\section{Experiments and meaning}
The fixed-policy audit uses uniform, adverse, rare-state/good-action,
common-state/bad-action, and near-optimal policies at
\begin{equation}
N\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256\}.
\end{equation}
Exact finite-$N$ moments establish ground truth; Monte Carlo validates the
formulas and measures nonlinear rank statistics.

The learning grid uses $\alpha=0.05$, 2000 updates,
$\beta\in\{0.01,0.1,0.2\}$, $N\in\{4,32,128\}$, and uniform and adverse
initializations. The full preset uses 100 paired seeds. Comparing sampled and
oracle conditional curves isolates the random-ratio effect.

Secondary experiments add one terminal Gaussian reward-noise draw with
standard deviation one or reproduce project return centering and normalization.
They remain secondary because they add variance or change gradient scale.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Execution status and reading rule}
The full preset completed all 128 declared configuration-method units with no
non-finite run. It produced 128 compressed trajectory archives and 58 plots.
Unless explicitly stated otherwise, endpoint numbers below are means over 100
paired seeds after 2000 updates. Confidence intervals are two-sided 95\%
Student-$t$ intervals across seeds.

The result should be read as an estimator study, not as a competition in which
every curve is equally implementable. In particular, the full pooled-Fisher
and visitation-only gradients use exact population information and are oracle
diagnostics. The practical candidate is
\texttt{detached\_conditional\_sampled}.

\subsection{Fixed-policy audit: what finite batches lose}
The adverse initialization has a rare $s_1$. Table~\ref{tab:audit} shows the
main mechanism. The mean conditional-gradient bias becomes small with $N$,
but the event $K_1=0$ remains common. Moreover, the undamped four-dimensional
empirical Fisher is often rank deficient, so its ordinary log-determinant is
undefined.

The distinction is important. The ratio estimator has a finite-$N$ mean bias,
but a more visible operational failure is intermittent protection: when
$K_1=0$, the sampled regularizer contributes exactly zero in the $s_1$ block.
The literal empirical log-determinant is therefore not a robust finite-batch
training object here unless one adds a separate stabilization assumption,
which this experiment intentionally does not do.

These endpoints expose the decomposition established in Step 3.
Visitation-only pressure raises $q$ but leaves the bad $s_1$ action policy

objective, not evidence that an ordinary neural rollout supplies the full
gradient.

\subsection{Favorable start and secondary bridges}
The barrier is not free. From the uniform initialization at $N=32$ and
$\beta=0.1$, reward-only reaches $J=0.97347$, while conditional sampled reaches
$0.79366$ and the full oracle reaches $0.79500$. Visitation-only reaches
$0.97500$. Thus a regularizer that helps escape the adverse basin can bias a
policy that is already learning the rewarding path. This is why results from
both prescribed initializations are necessary.

Adding terminal Gaussian noise with standard deviation one leaves the adverse
endpoint means qualitatively similar: reward-only $0.49068$, conditional
sampled $0.40018$, and full oracle $0.78350$, with wider uncertainty. In
contrast, project-style return centering and normalization at $N=128$ changes
the gradient scale: the corresponding endpoints are $0.49947$, $0.47884$, and
$0.96879$. The same numerical $\beta$ therefore represents a different
reward-to-regularizer balance after preprocessing and must be recalibrated in
a neural experiment.

\section{What the experiment establishes}
The finite-batch sampled conditional estimator is mathematically and
empirically characterized in this controlled problem. Its mean approaches the
population conditional target as $N$ increases, while rare-state batches can
remove direct protection of that state entirely. Its training behavior closely
approaches the conditional oracle with increasing $N$.

The experiment also confirms that three objects must remain distinct:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{s\sim\mu_\theta}[\nabla B_s],\qquad
\nabla\mathbb E_{s\sim\mu_\theta}[B_s],\qquad
\nabla\log\det F_{\mathrm{pool}}.
\end{equation}
The sampled conditional method estimates the first object. The complete
weighted oracle differentiates the second. The normalized full pooled-Fisher
oracle contains a separate visitation term. Their different returns do not by
themselves prove a causal effect of Fisher geometry, and a larger determinant
does not guarantee better conditioning.

\section{Statistics, reproduction, and decision gate}
Complete seeds are independent units. Curves use two-sided 95\% Student-$t$
intervals across seeds. Checkpoints within a seed are repeated observations;
endpoint comparisons use paired seed-level differences.

\begin{verbatim}
python -m exploration.sampled_tabular_mdp.verify
python -m exploration.sampled_tabular_mdp.run_experiment --preset smoke
python -m exploration.sampled_tabular_mdp.run_experiment --preset pilot
python -m exploration.sampled_tabular_mdp.run_experiment --preset full
\end{verbatim}
Outputs are stored under
\texttt{exploration/results/tabular\_mdp/two\_step\_trap\_sampled/}.

This stage can establish finite-batch behavior and its association with
learning. It cannot prove that Fisher volume causes return, make oracle
visitation gradients practical, or establish transfer to shared neural
parameters. The later neural candidate must be called an \emph{on-policy
sampled-state conditional categorical log barrier}, not a global neural Fisher
log-determinant.
